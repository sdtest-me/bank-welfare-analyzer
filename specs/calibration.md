# Calibration System

## Purpose

The calibration system exists to prevent analytical collapse into generic outputs.

Without calibration:
- different banks produce similar scores,
- narratives become repetitive,
- risk separation becomes weak,
- the model loses decision-making value.

Calibration ensures:
- meaningful differentiation,
- sensitivity to behavioral asymmetry,
- structural-risk visibility,
- narrative consistency.

---

# Core Calibration Objectives

## 1. Preserve Score Separation

Different institutions must produce visibly different:
- Impact Index values,
- structural risk levels,
- narratives,
- recommendations.

Target:
- weak banks should not cluster around average values,
- high-risk patterns must create strong divergence.

---

## 2. Prevent Narrative Repetition

Narratives must vary based on:
- dominant Spiral stages,
- mismatch pressure,
- extraction behavior,
- uncertainty level,
- impact severity.

The system should avoid:
- generic recommendation blocks,
- repeated warning language,
- static interpretation patterns.

---

## 3. Maintain Internal Consistency

The following outputs must align:
- scoring,
- impact,
- risk tier,
- recommendations,
- narrative framing.

Example:
- high impact risk cannot generate optimistic narratives,
- low-confidence inputs cannot produce deterministic conclusions.

---

# Calibration Principles

## Principle 1 — Structural Signals Override Cosmetic Signals

Behavioral contradictions matter more than PR language.

Example:
- ESG messaging cannot compensate for poverty-lending behavior.

---

## Principle 2 — Mismatch Increases Risk Nonlinearly

Mismatch escalation is exponential, not linear.

Small contradictions:
- informational concern.

Large contradictions:
- structural instability.

---

## Principle 3 — Missing Data Reduces Confidence

The system must:
- expose uncertainty,
- reduce analytical certainty,
- avoid deterministic outputs from weak inputs.

---

# Calibration Targets

## Desired Product Behavior

Users should:
- understand conclusions in under 60 seconds,
- immediately identify major risk drivers,
- perceive strong differentiation between institutions,
- recognize the product as decision-grade rather than academic.
