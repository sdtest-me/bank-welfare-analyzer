# Narrative Strategy

## Purpose

The narrative layer transforms analytical outputs into decision-readable interpretation.

The goal is not to describe data.

The goal is to explain:
- what the system means,
- why the risk matters,
- what consequences may emerge,
- what action should follow.

---

# Core Narrative Principles

## 1. Structural Interpretation Over Raw Metrics

The product should explain:
- behavioral meaning,
- institutional dynamics,
- systemic implications.

Not just numbers.

---

## 2. Risk Must Feel Real

Narratives should communicate:
- instability,
- extraction,
- fragility,
- governance pressure,
- social tension,
- institutional asymmetry.

Without emotional exaggeration.

---

## 3. Narrative Must Match Severity

Weak signals:
- measured language.

Strong structural contradictions:
- direct language,
- high urgency,
- explicit warnings.

---

# Narrative Architecture

## Narrative Inputs

Narratives are generated from:
- Impact Index,
- mismatch pressure,
- dominant Spiral stages,
- uncertainty/confidence,
- extraction indicators,
- calibration modifiers.

---

## Narrative Outputs

The system generates:
- executive summary,
- structural-risk interpretation,
- consequence framing,
- recommendations,
- confidence explanation,
- escalation warnings.

---

# Narrative Types

## Stable Development Narrative

Characteristics:
- balanced incentives,
- moderate extraction,
- adaptive structure,
- low mismatch.

Tone:
- constructive,
- analytical,
- low urgency.

---

## Transitional Narrative

Characteristics:
- emerging contradictions,
- uneven alignment,
- moderate instability.

Tone:
- cautionary,
- monitoring-oriented.

---

## Structural-Risk Narrative

Characteristics:
- aggressive extraction,
- high mismatch,
- unstable incentives,
- social pressure asymmetry.

Tone:
- direct,
- high urgency,
- consequence-oriented.

---

# Narrative Constraints

The system must avoid:
- generic consulting language,
- motivational tone,
- vague optimism,
- unsupported certainty,
- repetitive recommendation templates.

---

# Decision-Speed Requirement

Users should understand:
- the core risk,
- the dominant driver,
- the strategic implication,

within 30–60 seconds.

---

# Conversion Role

Narrative is also a conversion mechanism.

Strong narratives increase:
- perceived analytical depth,
- product credibility,
- Sponsor Lab conversion potential.

Weak narratives reduce:
- trust,
- differentiation,
- perceived value.

---

# Long-Term Direction

Future narrative evolution may include:
- regulator-focused narratives,
- investor narratives,
- ESG narratives,
- country-specific interpretation layers,
- simulation narratives,
- scenario-based futures.
